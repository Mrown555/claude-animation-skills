# How to teach (narration and structure)

The viewer is an MBA student meeting the idea for the first time. They should understand it by watching once.

## Lesson shape (5 to 10 min)
1. **Hook (title):** the business question this lesson answers ("How much should a business order each time?").
2. **Concept:** the definition from the notes, then the same idea in plain words.
3. **Why it exists:** the problem it solves, with an everyday picture (a kirana store, a chai stall, a Swiggy delivery, a college canteen). Mark your own examples as supplementary.
4. **How it works:** step by step, one scene per step. For formulas: each quantity, their relationship on a chart, the formula term by term, then the PDF's worked example line by line.
5. **Business use, advantages, limitations:** from the notes first; label any additions.
6. **Recap:** 3 to 4 lines the student must remember, including the example's answer.

## Narration rules
- Short spoken sentences, 8 to 25 words, one idea each. Use "you" and "we".
- Explain every technical term the first time ("lead time, which is the number of days the supplier takes to deliver"), and keep the term itself.
- Say where things come from: "your notes define…", "Example 1 on page five…". When adding something: "here is an example of my own, not from your notes."
- Read calculations as a teacher would: what we substitute, what we get, and what it means.
- Compare related concepts explicitly (EOQ vs ROP: how much vs when).
- Indian context is welcome for illustration (₹, kirana, godown, Diwali demand), but never attach invented figures to real companies. Real company facts only if they are in the PDF.

## Never
- Invent statistics, formulas, case facts or numbers. Generic illustrative numbers in a supplementary scene must be obviously hypothetical ("say, 100 packets a week").
- Read the PDF aloud word for word.
- Skip a hard section. If it can't be read, say so in the outline and to the user.
